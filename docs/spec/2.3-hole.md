# 2.3 Hole

## 1. Purpose

Hole drills standard holes: simple, for a bolt (clearance), threaded (tapped) and with a
taper thread (taper tapped). A hole can have a seat at the top: a cylindrical counterbore,
a spotface or a conical countersink.
What matters is that the hole keeps its full *description*: type, standard, size, thread,
class, seat, depths, drill point. That is why a drawing can write the note by itself
("4x M8x1.25 - 6H ↧ 12") and a hole table can count them.

## 2. Inputs

- **Positions:** the hole centres.
  - A click on a planar face creates a sketch on the face with a centre point under the
    cursor (Allow Center Point Creation, on by default).
  - Points of an existing sketch.
  - Concentric to a circular edge.
- **Solids:** which body, when there is more than one.
- **Type:** the hole type and the seat type.
- **Behavior:** termination (Distance / Through All / To), direction, drill point.
- **Threads** (for threads): standard, size, designation (pitch), class, thread depth,
  right/left hand, modeled thread, clearance for 3D printing.
- **Fastener** (for clearance): standard, fastener type, size, fit (Close /
  Normal / Loose).

The standards values are read from easily edited tables (CSV) in `data/standards/` (built
into the program). A user file with the same name in `%APPDATA%\3dcadcraft\standards\`
(Linux `~/.config/3dcadcraft/standards/`) replaces the built-in one. The numbers are stored
in the feature itself, so the part regenerates the same on every machine, whatever tables
are there.

| File | Contents | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `iso_metric_threads.csv` | ISO metric threads, coarse and fine pitch: designation, D, P, drill, D1, classes | ISO 261/262; D1 = D − 1.082532·P (ISO 724), drill = D − P |
| `unified_threads.csv` | UNC / UNF: size, TPI, D, drill, D1, classes | ASME B1.1; drill for a 75 % profile = D − 0.9743/TPI |
| `clearance_holes.csv` | bolt holes Close / Normal / Loose | ISO 273 (fine/medium/coarse), ASME B18.2.8 |
| `counterbores.csv` | counterbores for socket head cap screws | DIN 974-1 for ISO 4762, ASME B18.3 |
| `countersinks.csv` | countersinks | ISO 10642 (90°), ASME B18.3 (82°) |

## 4. Browser and editing behaviour

- In the browser it is `Hole1`, and its sketch is under it (created automatically on a
  click on a face, hidden). A hole with only concentric centres has no sketch.
- Edit Feature opens the same panel. The model is shown as it was before the hole.
  A failed edit keeps the old definition. Centres are moved in the hole's sketch
  (Edit Sketch), not in the panel.
- Face names contain the hole number `k` (from 1, in the order of the centres):
  `Hole1:Bore:k`, `Hole1:Bottom:k`, `Hole1:Tip:k`, `Hole1:CBore:k`, `Hole1:CBoreFloor:k`,
  `Hole1:Spotface:k`, `Hole1:SpotfaceFloor:k`, `Hole1:CSink:k`, `Hole1:Thread:k`.
- Properties of a selected hole: type, termination, centres, thread, the note and the
  values with expressions (parameters `diameter`, `depth`, `point_angle`, `seat_diameter`,
  `seat_depth`, `csink_diameter`, `csink_angle`, `thread_depth`, `thread_clearance`
  depending on the settings).

## 5. Edge cases and errors

- No centres: "Hole: select the hole centres".
- Diameter ≤ 0, depth ≤ 0: a message and a sick feature; the part does not change.
- A seat narrower than the hole, or a counterbore / countersink deeper than the hole: a message.
- A thread deeper than the hole: a message.
- A hole that does not intersect the body: "Cut: the feature does not intersect the body".
- A To face that is not planar: "not supported yet"; parallel to the hole or behind it:
  a message.
- A hole on a curved face: the sketch for the centres needs a planar face, so it is
  refused (test `hole_on_a_curved_face_is_refused`).
- A modeled thread over 200 turns: a message to use a cosmetic one.
- **The modeled thread and the exact kernel.** The exact kernel's Boolean with the smooth
  helical surface (`sk::sweep_helix`, B-spline faces) was measured as too slow: 2.5 s for
  half a turn through the hole, over 60 s once the groove crosses a plane, 8 turns did not
  finish in 15 min. That is why the groove is cut as a triangle mesh (`sweep_helix_mesh` +
  `faceted_boolean`, as in SolidCraft), but **only in the zone around the thread**: the body
  is split into an exact part (the body without the ring around the thread) and the zone;
  only the zone is faceted. The two parts touch along the ring's wall. Threads are cut after
  all features so that no later Boolean reaches the faceted zone (a later feature that fills
  the hole would be cut up — a rare case). The volume is within 1–2 % of the analytic one
  (36 sections per turn, the hole in the zone is a polygon with a 0.01 mm deviation).
  `kernel::exact::helix_tool` (the smooth face) remains for Coil and a future faster path.
- **"The thread generates no geometry" (owner's check, 2026-10-19).** Two causes:
  1. The groove was matched to the body by the middle of its zone. The top end of the zone
     followed the tool's clearance above the face (1 % of the part size), so with a thin
     plate on a large part the middle fell above the plate. The groove was then skipped
     without any message. Now the matching uses the box of the whole zone, and the groove
     reaches only one pitch above the face.
  2. A thread that no body contains becomes a sick feature with a reason instead of
     silently disappearing.
- **The zone in slabs (2026-10-20, the owner's session).** M8 with a modeled thread, 10 deep
  in a 283 × 136 × 10 plate, failed after 150 s ("shell is not oriented and closed"). Three
  causes:
  1. Distance depth = the thickness: the groove stopped "square" with its lowest corners
     exactly on the bottom face. Now, once the hole wall reaches the other side of the body,
     the groove runs out as with Through All.
  2. The faceted operation grows faster than the groove length: the whole zone at once — 13.6 s.
     Now the zone is cut in **slabs** along the axis (2 turns each), each slab with the part
     of the groove that reaches it, in parallel (`kernel::exact::slab_threads`): 1.6 s (release).
  3. A slab cut out of a very large body came out inexact (planes tilted by 1.7e-6 rad,
     circles shifted by 1 µm — the kernel's tolerances follow the size of the operands).
     Such a slab is cut again from the body within a box around the zone. This way M3 in a
     300 × 300 × 3 plate (the previously known limitation) is cut too.
  A slab whose faceted operation fails (this depends on where exactly its plane lies —
  close to a face of the body or to a row of facets) is cut again with shifted planes, then
  merged with its neighbour; in the worst case the zone is one piece, as before.
- **A hole that reaches the other side** (Distance ≥ the thickness): the bottom and the
  drill point are in the air and cut nothing, but a cap exactly on the bottom face or a cone
  outside the material sent the kernel down the slow general path (283 mm plate: 6.1 s,
  sometimes an invalid result). The tool goes through the body as with Through All (9 faces
  instead of 12, the same volume).
- If the exact kernel gives up on a hole (and the part is built faceted), the hole's health
  shows the real reason ("the geometry kernel failed: …"), not "Hole needs the exact
  geometry kernel".
- Known SolidCraft issues: #2038 (a modeled thread facets the whole body — ours only the
  zone, test), #2705/#2969 (thread diameter tolerance — ours keeps the diameters from the
  table, the clearance is a separate field, Thread checks that the size fits the face),
  #559 (a Ø1 hole in the 2 mm edge of a shelled box — test in the matrix, passes).

## 6. Implementation

- `part::standards`: the CSV tables (built in with `include_str!` + a user folder,
  `Standards::load`, `Standards::global`), a malformed row → a warning `file:line`.
- `part::hole`: `HoleFeature` (`Feature::Hole`, format revision 6), `HoleSpec`, `ThreadSpec`,
  `HoleCenter`, `HoleSpec::tools` (the tool profiles), `note()` / `note_line()` /
  `note_ascii()`, `HoleInstance` with `transformed(&Matrix4)` (link for Pattern / Mirror),
  `group_holes`; `PartDocument::add_hole`, `create_hole_sketch`, `holes`, `hole_groups`,
  `hole_placements`.
- `part::exact::hole`: each centre gives tools of revolution (`kernel::exact::revolved_tool`):
  first the seats of all holes (one Boolean), then the bores through their floors
  (a second one). One stepped tool per hole made 36 counterbores take 43 s, the two stages
  1.6 s. The tools start above the face (in the air), their seams are rotated.
- Modeled thread: `kernel::exact::cut_threads` (zone + faceted groove, in parallel by
  zone), `part::exact::finish_threads` after all features (cached).
- GUI: `gui/hole.rs` (picking in the view, preview, OK/Apply, the cosmetic thread in the
  view), `gui/hole_panel.rs` (the panels and Properties).
- Tests: `tests/holes.rs` (every type and seat, threads, grouping, editing, 36 holes),
  `tests/matrix.rs` (hole + fillet / chamfer, Shell + hole in the edge #559, hole + Shell,
  editing), `kernel::exact::hole_tests`, `part::hole::tests`, `part::standards::tests`,
  `tests/perf.rs`, fixture `tests/compat/r6/hole_r6.3dcadcraft`.
