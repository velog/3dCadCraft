# 6.2 Design calculators: shafts, keys, gears, bearings, springs

Status: ✅ done · 🟡 partial · ⬜ to do · ⛔ blocked by another feature.

The generators of the Design tab that **calculate** a machine element and **model** it (Inventor's Design
Accelerator, FEATURES §6.2). Bolted Connection has its own spec ([6.2-bolted-connection.md](6.2-bolted-connection.md));
this file covers the others. Every generator follows the same split:

- a **calculation**: pure functions in `src/design/` (no GUI, no kernel), each formula with its source in the
  rustdoc (document, section, equation number), unit-tested against the source's own worked examples;
- a **model**: an ordinary part (and its occurrence in the assembly) built from the generator's values, with
  the persistent face names of its builder;
- the **panel**: the Design tab's planned panel (rule 15), OK enabled once the logic exists (rule 18).

**State (2026-11-10):** sources chosen (§0); calculations of the shaft (RP-1123), the parallel key (Kimball and
Barr) and spur / helical gear geometry and surface life (RP-1152) in `src/design/` with the sources' worked
examples as tests; the Shaft generator's Design / Calculation pages with OK building the shaft part. Bearings and
springs: ⬜.

## 0. Sources and licences

The owner's requirement (2026-11-10): every formula comes from a trustworthy document that is **legally free to
read**, cited where it is used. Commercial standards (ISO 6336, DIN 743, ISO 281, DIN 3990, AGMA 2001) are not
free: we use them only for the *names* of methods and say where our free source's method differs.

No single free, current textbook covers every element with a clear licence (the open-textbook libraries list
none for machine design; NPTEL course notes state no clear licence). The best free sources are the **NASA
Reference Publications** of the Lewis Research Center, written for designers by the people who ran NASA's
drive-train research, each with worked examples. As works of the United States Government they are not
protected by copyright in the US (17 U.S.C. §105) and are distributed by NASA with *Distribution: Public*.
Where NASA has no design text (keys, springs) we use a classic textbook that is in the public domain.

Why these: they are primary sources of a government research centre (RP-1123 is the basis of the ASME B106.1M
shaft equation, RP-1152 the Lundberg–Palmgren life model NASA verified on its own gear tests), they carry worked
examples with numbers our tests can check, and nobody can forbid reading or using them. Their age is the price:

- **Shafts.** RP-1123 corrects the fatigue limit of a polished specimen by factors (surface, size, reliability,
  temperature, notch, press fit, residual stress, corrosion) and sizes the shaft by the distortion-energy
  ellipse of ASME B106.1M (eq. 17 / 18). DIN 743 (the method of Inventor's European setting) uses its own
  notch and size factors and an equivalent mean stress; its numbers differ by up to some tens of per cent. We
  say "per NASA RP-1123" in the results.
- **Gears.** RP-1152 gives the geometry (eq. 1–12), the AGMA 218.01 form of the bending stress (eq. 53) with
  factors from charts, and the NASA surface-fatigue life (eq. 65–75). ISO 6336 / DIN 3990 compute bending and
  pitting *safety factors* with their own factor sets; we give a stress and a life instead and name the
  method "Lewis / AGMA 218.01 per NASA RP-1152".
- **Keys.** Kimball and Barr take the key's bearing on half its height (§69 eq. 3); for a DIN 6885 key we take
  the part that stands in the hub (h − t1), the usual modern reading of the same equation.
- **Bearings.** RP-1105 gives the basic life L10 = (C/P)^p of ISO 281's basic rating; ISO 281's modified life
  (a_ISO) is not in it and stays out.

The PDFs are scans. Our tests check the numbers of the worked examples; the code cites equations, never copies
text or tables (rule: own words; single numbers only).

## 1. Purpose

Machine elements are drawn many times a day and sized by formulas everybody repeats by hand. A generator asks
for the few values that decide the element, checks it against its loads, and places a finished part in the
assembly, so the designer changes numbers instead of sketches.

## 2. Inputs

- **Shaft**: an axis (cylindrical face or work axis) and a start plane in the assembly (optional: the origin);
  the sections from left to right; for the calculation the material, the supports and the loads.
- **Key**: the shaft's cylindrical face and a start plane, the key's standard, the torque.
- **Spur gears**: module, pressure angle, helix angle, teeth and face widths of both gears, profile shifts,
  the placement (cylindrical face and start plane per gear); for the calculation power and speed (or torque),
  the material, the factors.

## 4. Browser and editing

The generated shaft is an ordinary part (one `BodyOp::Axial` feature) placed as an occurrence; its name is
`Shaft<n>`. Editing it again through the generator (Inventor: Edit using Design Accelerator) needs the
generator's values stored with the part: ⬜ (planned: an optional `design` field, additive, rule 6).

## 5. Edge cases and errors

- No section: `Shaft: add a section first`. A chamfer as large as the step, a groove deeper than the radius,
  a polygon with fewer than 3 sides: `Shaft: <section k>: <reason>`; nothing is made (rule 5).
- Calculation with fewer or more than two supports: `Shaft: the calculation needs exactly two supports`.
  A support or load outside the shaft: `Shaft: the load at z = … lies outside the shaft`.
- Key: no DIN 6885 key for the diameter: `Key: no DIN 6885 key for a Ø… shaft (6 … 230 mm)`.
- Gears: teeth below 5, a negative operating pressure angle (too much negative shift), a contact ratio < 1:
  the result's warnings.

## 6. Implementation

- `src/design/mod.rs`: the module; `src/design/shaft.rs` the sections → `AxialOp` (faces `<id>:S<k>:Wall`,
  `:Chamfer`, `:Fillet`, `:Groove`, caps `Start` / `End`); `src/design/shaft_calc.rs` the beam (shear, moment,
  slope, deflection by integrating M / EI over the divisions; supports by statics) and RP-1123's equations;
  `src/design/key.rs`; `src/design/gear.rs`.
- GUI: `gui/shaft.rs` the Shaft panel's section list and Calculation page on top of the planned panel
  (`PartDialog::shaft`), OK = `design::shaft::part` placed through `assembly::create::place_part`.
