# 6.1 Frames (Frame Generator)

> **Specification before the interface** (rule 15). Every row starts ⬜. Rows marked *(to confirm)* are
> not described in the 2026 / 2027 help pages read so far; check them before their logic is written.
> Weldments, which frames usually end up in, are [6.4](6.4-weldments.md); the profile library is

## 1. Purpose

Steel structures (machine frames, racks, platforms, guards, trailers) built from standard sections —
angles, channels, I / H beams, T sections, square / rectangular / round tubes, flats, round bars — laid
along the lines of a **skeleton** and then cut to fit where they meet (mitre, corner joint, notch, trim
to a face, lengthen / shorten). Each member is an ordinary part whose length and cuts are known, so:

- the BOM lists *"EN 10219 – 40×40×3 – 1 250 mm"* with the length as the quantity;
- drawings get a **cut list** (length, cut angles at both ends, quantity, mass);
- the frame changes when the skeleton changes: a longer line → a longer member, end cuts re-solved.

## 2. Inputs

- An **assembly** (Inventor: a normal assembly or a weldment, 6.4).
- A **skeleton**: one or more components whose geometry the members follow —
  - 2D sketch lines, arcs, splines, ellipse arcs; 3D sketch lines / arcs / splines (⛔ 1.10 3D sketch);
  - model edges (straight, arc, spline, ellipse arc) of a part;
  - points: sketch points, vertices, work points (for *Between Points*).
  Several skeleton components may be used at once; a member may run between geometry of two of them.
- A **frame member family** from the library (6.3 L3): Category › Standard › Family › Size, plus
  Material and Appearance.

## 3. Options

All Frame commands live on **Design › Frame** in the Assembly and Weldment environments (3.0) and are
property panels (Inventor 2021–2024 moved them from dialogs): a presets row, an **Advanced Settings**
menu in the panel's upper right corner, Apply (keeps the panel open) and OK / Cancel. Every value field
is an expression field (rule 13) with an in-view manipulator (rule 12). The commands that change members
offer **Delete existing end treatments** in Advanced Settings (on by default *(to confirm)*).

## 4. Browser and editing behaviour

- The assembly lists a **Frame** subassembly (`Frame1`) with the skeleton component(s) and the members
  (`<Stock Number>-<n>`); end treatments are listed under each member (*Miter 1*, *Notch 2*…), the
  Driven Length under the member's own feature tree (Explode shows it).
- Moving or editing a skeleton line regenerates the member and every end treatment it takes part in.
- A member whose skeleton curve was deleted keeps its last shape and gets health (*"Frame member: its
  skeleton line is gone"*), like a sick sketch (rule 5, rule 8).
- Members are not dragged: they are placed by their skeleton (no free DOF); Free Move refuses them.

## 5. Edge cases and errors

- Mitre of parallel or non-meeting members: `Miter: the members do not meet.`
- Corner Joint where the blue member does not reach the yellow one's plane even extended (parallel):
  `Corner Joint: the members are parallel.`
- Trim/Extend whose face does not cross the member's line: `Trim/Extend: the face does not reach the member.`
- Notch whose tool does not touch the member: `Notch: the members do not touch.`
- Lengthen/Shorten below zero length: refused (`Lengthen/Shorten: the member would vanish.`).
- A size missing from the library after an update: the member keeps the copied section data (rule 8);
  Refresh reports it.
- A spline skeleton with a curvature radius smaller than the section's half height: health (the sweep
  folds), never a self-intersecting solid.
- Merge of a chain that is not tangent-continuous: refused, or members joined by a mitre *(to confirm)*.

## 6. Implementation (proposal)

### 6.1 Data

- **Profile library** (6.3 L3): `data/profiles/<standard>-<family>.csv` with the standard's dimension
  columns (h, b, tw, tf, r1, r2, t…), typed from the standards themselves (EN 10056-1 angles, EN 10279 UPN
  channels, EN 10365 IPE / HEA / HEB, EN 10219 / 10210 hollow sections, EN 10058 flats, EN 10060 rounds,
  ASTM A6 W shapes…) — never from a commercial CAD library. Each family names a **section template**
  (our code: `part::frame::section::Shape` — `Angle`, `Channel`, `IBeam`, `Tee`, `RectTube`, `RoundTube`,
  `Flat`, `Bar`, `Custom(sketch)`) that builds the sketch (lines + arcs, constrained, `redundant == 0`) and
  the section properties (A, Ix, Iy, centroid) in closed form.
- **Member part**: a `PartDocument` with a new `Feature::FrameMember` (format revision): the section data
  *copied in* (rule 8), the path as explicit geometry in the part's coordinates (polyline / arc / spline
  copied from the skeleton, re-written by the frame solver), orientation (anchor, offsets, angle, mirror),
  and the end treatments as **resolved cut data** (planes, notch tool shapes as copied loops). The part
  regenerates from its own features only; the frame solver writes new data into the part when the
  skeleton or a neighbour changes (like Inventor's Frame Generator writing into member files).
- **Frame definition** in the assembly: `AssemblyDocument::frames: Vec<Frame>` — skeleton references as
  `(occurrence path, persistent name)` (rule 8: sketch entity ids, edge names), member definitions
  (family key, size, orientation, the member's `DocRef`), end treatments between member ids
  (`Miter { a, b, kind, gap, offset }`, `CornerJoint`, `TrimToFace { face: GeomRef }`, `Notch`,
  `Lengthen`). Ids never reused (like occurrences).
- **Frame solve** (`assembly::frame::solve`): for each member, resolve its skeleton curve in the frame's
  coordinates → section frame along the curve → length; then end treatments in creation order, each as
  planes / tool loops in the member's local coordinates. Pure, GUI-free, tested without the GUI.

### 6.2 Geometry

- Straight member: Extrude of the section by `G_L` (the fast, exact path); arc: Revolve; spline / merged
  chain: Sweep along the path (5.1) with the section kept square to the path.
- Mitre / Corner Joint / Trim / Lengthen = planar cuts at the ends: built into the member's extrusion as
  inclined terminations (`extrude::Clip`, already exact) — no Boolean per member.
- Notch = Cut of the member by the other member's section grown by the offset (Basic) or the template's
  offsets (I / C / T), extruded along the other member's axis; Perpendicular Cut projects the outline
  square to the notched member. One Boolean per notch, tools of one member joined by `union_apart`.
- Topology names: `<member id>:Start`, `:End`, `:Side<k>` (from the template's curve roles), notch faces
  `<member id>:Notch<k>:…`.

### 6.3 GUI

- `gui/frame.rs`: the Insert / Change panel on the `command_ui` flow (selectors: Input Geometry, Align),
  anchor buttons + offset arrows + rotation arc as `manip.rs` handles, preview on the worker thread.
- End treatment panels as `asm_planned` pages first (rule 15), then logic row by row.
- Right-click menus (rule 17): a sketch line / edge in an assembly offers Insert Frame; a member offers
  Change, Miter, Corner Joint, Notch, Trim/Extend, Lengthen/Shorten, Remove End Treatments, Frame Member
  Info, Edit with Frame Generator.

### 6.4 Tests (each stage)

- Section properties vs the standard's tables (A, Ix, Iy of IPE 200, L 50×50×5, RHS 40×40×3).
- Member volume = A·L (straight), Pappus (arc); length after mitre / joint vs hand calculation;
  `CUTDETAIL` angles.
- Moving a skeleton line changes length and keeps end treatments; deleting it gives health.
- Save / load round trip; old builds refuse the new feature (compat fixture for the revision).
- Inventor as the reference for cut lengths and mass of a few frames (outside CI).
- Perf: a 200-member frame regenerates within its budget (`tests/perf.rs`).
