# 6.1 Frames (Frame Generator)

Status: ✅ done · 🟡 partial · ⬜ to do · ⛔ blocked by another feature.

> **Research document first** (the owner's rule, 2026-10-24). Every row ⬜; *(to confirm)* rows are written
> from general Inventor knowledge and checked against the 2027 help before they are implemented.

## 1. Purpose

Steel structures (frames, racks, platforms) from standard profiles — angles, channels, I / H beams, tubes,
flats — placed along the lines of a skeleton, then cut to fit at the joints (mitre, trim, notch). Each
member is a part with its length known, so the BOM lists "IPE 200 × 1 250 mm" and drawings get cut lists.

## 2. Inputs

- An assembly with a **skeleton**: a part with 2D / 3D sketch lines, work points or model edges.
- A profile from the standard library (6.3): standard (ISO / DIN / ANSI / EN…), family, size, material.

## 4. Browser and editing behaviour

- The frame sub-assembly lists the members; the skeleton drives them: moving a skeleton line moves the
  member; end treatments re-solve.
- A member whose skeleton line is gone keeps its last shape and is marked (as "health").

## 5. Edge cases and errors

- A mitre between members that do not meet, or parallel ones: refused with `Miter: …`.
- A profile size missing from the library after an update: the member keeps its data (copied in, rule 8).

## 6. Implementation (research)

- **Profiles** come from the library of standard components (6.3): a family table (CSV like our
  `data/standards/*.csv`) per standard and shape with the section's dimensions; the profile sketch is
  built from the shape's template (lines + arcs with the table's values), never from Autodesk data.
- **A member** is a generated part: the profile swept / extruded along its skeleton segment (our Extrude
  or Sweep, 2.1 / 5.1), length as a parameter; end treatments are Cut / Split features by planes or by
  the other member (our Booleans, 2.13). Names: `<family> <size> - <n>`.
- **Frame data** in the assembly: the skeleton references `(occurrence, persistent name)` of the lines
  (rule 8), the member definitions and the joints (mitre, trim, notch) — re-run when the skeleton changes.
- SolidCraft has no frame generator; its sweep and Boolean paths are what we already use.
