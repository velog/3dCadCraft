# 7.2 Dynamic Simulation

Status: ✅ done · 🟡 partial · ⬜ to do · ⛔ waiting for another feature.

## 1. Purpose

Inventor's **Dynamic Simulation** environment (Environments → Dynamic Simulation) moves an assembly's
components in time under gravity, springs, motors and external loads, held together by joints. It
reports positions, velocities, accelerations, joint reactions and energies over time (the Output
Grapher), plays the motion back, and can hand the joint loads of one instant to stress analysis.
Unlike a drag (kinematics, `assembly::solve`) it uses the components' masses and inertias.

## 2. Inputs

- An assembly with joints (spec 3.1 §3.3: Rigid, Rotational, Slider, Cylindrical, Planar, Ball).
- Each moving component's mass properties: the part's Physical Properties (material density, spec 2.15).
- Gravity, joint springs / dampers, motors (imposed motion or effort), loads.
- Simulation settings: end time, number of images (output interval), solver tolerance.

## 4. Browser and editing

Inventor keeps a Dynamic Simulation browser (Mobile Groups, Grounded, Standard Joints, Rolling /
Sliding / Contact / Force joints, External Loads, Gravity) and saves its settings with the assembly.
⬜ Our simulations will be stored in `AssemblyDocument` (like `simulations` of Frame Analysis), the
results never saved (computed again).

## 5. Edge cases and errors

- Nothing moves (every component grounded or without joints): `Dynamic Simulation: no component moves…`.
- A moving part without mass (no solid, or a material without density): the error names the component.
- A spring or load on something that is not simulated: an error naming it.
- The solver's own refusals (singular joint equations, divergence, step too small, step limit) are
  errors with the time where they happened; the model is never changed by a run (rule 5).

## 6. Implementation

- Solver: SolidCraft `crates/dynamics` (main `61351c67`), copied whole into `vendor/solidcraft-dynamics`
  (rigid bodies in maximal coordinates, Lagrange multipliers in index-1 form, Runge–Kutta 4 or
  Dormand–Prince 5(4), projection onto the joints); units mm, kg, s, mN.
- `assembly::dynamics`: `simulate(asm, local, mass, settings)` maps occurrences, joints, masses
  (Physical Properties in g and g·mm² → kg, kg·mm²; each body's axes are its part's, origin its
  centre of gravity), springs and loads to the solver's model and returns `Run` (samples of
  placements per occurrence, joint values, energy balance, joint error); `simulate_in(project, asm,
  settings)` reads parts and masses as the app does. Joint origins come from `joint::frame_of`; the
  second origin's z is turned round unless the joint is flipped (`aligned`), because the solver's
  frames coincide where ours face each other.
- Sample: `assembly::dynamics::pendulum` (a bar hanging on a Rotational joint beside a grounded post).
  Test `pendulum_swings_with_the_compound_period`: the period matches T = 2π √(I_pivot / (m g d))
  within 0.2 %, the pin stays on the hinge, the energy balance holds.
