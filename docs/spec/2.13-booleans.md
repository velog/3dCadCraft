# 2.13 Booleans and multiple bodies (Join / Cut / Intersect)

Status: ✅ done · 🟡 partial · ⬜ to do · ⛔ waiting for another feature.

## 1. Purpose

Extrude, Revolve (and later Hole, Sweep, Loft) do not only create bodies. They **add** material to an existing body (**Join**), **cut** from it (**Cut**) or **keep the common part** (**Intersect**). That is how bosses, pockets, holes and grooves are made in one part.

Body identity: a body is named after **the feature that created it** (immutable `id`, like the faces), not by its order in memory — otherwise the "Solids" selection would get shuffled on regeneration (rule 2).

## 3. Representation (stage 1 — faceting)

Exact B-Rep Booleans (intersecting planes, cylinders, cones and NURBS, decision K11) are a large separate task. **Stage 1** gives full functionality with this representation:
- every body is broken into polygons; curved faces are approximated with a tolerance of 0.01 mm;
- the Boolean is **BSP CSG** on the polygons;
- every polygon **remembers the face it comes from**: name and exact surface;
- the result is assembled back into a closed B-Rep:
  - the fragments of one planar face are merged into one face with holes;
  - T-junctions are repaired so the shell is a manifold;
  - faces that are facets of a curved surface remember their surface (`Face::facet_of`) and are drawn smooth, without the edges between the facets.

Consequences:
- names are kept (`Extrusion1:End`), so sketches on faces and projections work after Cut and Join too;
- curved faces are polygonal within the tolerance. Exact curved edges after a Boolean come in **stage 2** (exact Booleans).

## 4. Implementation

`kernel/csg.rs` (BSP, tagged polygons), `kernel/facet.rs` (`BRep::boolean`, `solid_polygons`, `add_polygons`: welding, T-junctions, merging planar fragments, removing redundant collinear vertices, splitting connected bodies, dropping pieces without volume), `part/boolean.rs` (`Operation`, `Output`, `apply` at regeneration).

Limitations of stage 1: coincident curved faces on **different** axes or with a different discretisation leave thin "wedges"; Join works on the first body.
