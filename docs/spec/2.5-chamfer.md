# 2.5 Chamfer (bevelling edges)

Status: ✅ done · 🟡 partial · ⬜ to do · ⛔ waiting for another feature.

## 1. Purpose

Bevels the edges of the body: cuts at a convex edge, adds material at a concave one.

## 2b. Chamfer larger than the radius of an arc in the loop

Case: the top loop of a box with rounded vertical edges (r), a chamfer with d > r along
the whole loop. Inventor and Onshape refuse; the advice is to change the order (chamfer
the sharp corners first, then the rounds). Creo has an **Intersect** transition: the adjacent pieces of the chamfer
are extended until they meet in a sharp edge. In ours the top face always gets sharp
corners (the loop offset inwards by d). The **Cone at small rounds** checkbox chooses what happens
below them:

| Checkbox | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| cleared (the default) — **B** | the chamfer planes are extended through the corner and cut the cylinder of the round; above it they meet in a sharp edge (like Creo Intersect, like the order "chamfer, then round") | ✅ model, test (volume against the integral), visual (d = 6 with r = 5) |
| set — **A** | below the corner the chamfer follows the arc as a cone until the cone shrinks to an apex; above the apex the planes meet in a sharp edge (the loop is offset inwards at every height) | ⬜ truck's Booleans do not cut a tangent cone and plane. The checkbox is in the dialog and in Properties; when a cone is needed the feature gets the warning "Cone at small rounds is not supported yet…" and is not applied (checked visually) |

Applies only when the edges are the whole convex outer loop of a planar face with perpendicular
walls, and the kernel's own chamfer refuses. With d ≤ r both are the same (the chamfer follows the arc).

## 3. Implementation

Field `BlendFeature::cone_corners` (default `false`). `part/exact.rs::loop_blend`: when the kernel's chamfer refuses and the edges are the whole convex loop of a planar face with perpendicular walls, every straight piece gets a wedge (a triangle whose long side is the chamfer line, extended past both faces; `chamfer_wedge`), extended past its ends. Arcs with r larger than the chamfer get a revolved wedge; smaller ones (B) nothing. The new faces are named `Chamfer1:Bevel[<the two faces>]`.

Shared module with Fillet (`part/blend.rs`). The profile is a triangle (the edge, a point on face 1, a point on face 2). It is built as for Fillet (prism or ring), without an arc.
