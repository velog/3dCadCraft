# Where the component tables were checked (2026-11-02, 2026-11-03)

The values were typed for this library and then compared, row by row, with the public pages below; they were
not copied from them in bulk, and nothing comes from commercial CAD. A row of `families.csv` names what its table
was compared with; an empty `checked` means **not compared yet**. Nothing here replaces the standard itself: check
a value against the standard or the manufacturer before relying on it in production.

**When sources disagree, both values stay** (owner's rule, 2026-11-03): makers and editions really differ. The
second value is a row of its own, named after the difference: `M10 (s 16)`, `R6 (B 5.558)`. A value only we had
typed, which a source contradicts, is simply corrected.

Corrected, a typed value against a source:

- DIN 933 and DIN 931 head heights (they are the ISO ones; only some across flats differ), ISO 10642 head heights.
- ISO 4162: every across flats, head height and flange diameter (they mixed two series; now those of EN 1662, derived from ISO 4162).
- ISO 8752 size 6 wall; DIN 660 sizes 3, 6 head; DIN 7349 sizes 10, 16, 20; DIN 705 sizes 6, 40, 50; bearing HK1210 outside diameter (16).
- ASME B18.3: socket depths of the cap screws, heads of the flat countersunk screws, head of the No.10 button screw. ASME B18.21.1: outside diameters.
- Lengths the standards do not list are gone from ISO 4026 to 4029, ISO 2338, DIN 7, 913 and 916 (an M12 x 4 set screw, with an 8 mm socket).
- ANSI B17.1: which shaft takes which key (every typed range was wrong). ASME B16.11 couplings: every length. Carriages: every height (the typed value left out the rail's part). V-belt pulleys: rim widths, SPA and SPB grooves, the wider groove above 80, 118 and 190 mm. Sprockets: tooth widths and every bore. Ball valves: lengths and heights. Hose clamps: a 9 mm band and 0.6 mm throughout, by range.
- `ISO 3601` was renamed `Metric O-Ring`: its round sizes are common trade sizes, not that series (sections 1.78, 2.62, 3.53, 5.33, 6.99).

Other findings:

- ISO 4017 M30 / M36: 18.7 / 22.5 is the middle of the standard's limits; one page rounds them to 19 / 23.
- Set screws: socket depth `t` depends on the length; the tables hold the long screws' value.
- ASME B18.21.1 1/2: the page repeats the row of 5/8, so this size was not compared.

Types added 2026-11-03 (DIN 84, 85, 94, 434, 508, 557, 603, 661, 929, 937, 938, 981, 1444, 1473, 2093, 5406, 6319, 6797, 6799, 6886) were
written from the fasten.it pages, each `checked` cell saying so. Their shapes leave some details out for now, which the numbers do not depend
on: screw slots, a carriage bolt's square neck, castle slots, washer teeth, a taper washer's slope, a stud's plain middle, and a cotter
pin is a plain pin; `spec/6.3-content-center.md` §6.5b keeps the list.

DIN 315, 444, 478 and ISO 7379 give limits: the tables hold the largest value of each. DIN 7337's lengths are common trade lengths; a typing slip on its page (`K 1.1.0` at Ø5) is read as 1.10.

Compared only in part: HK2516 and HK3016 (the name gives bore and width, not the outside diameter), DIN 179 (sizes 6 and 12), SAE (1/4, 1/2) and USS (1/2) washers; ISO 2339, DIN 975 and 976, UNC rods: diameters and pitches, not lengths. ASME B18.2.2 lists no No.10 nut.

Makers' ranges standing in for a standard's table, which is sold: DIN 3760 (M Seals), ISO 3547 (Permaglide / SKF). A compression spring's rows are those of 5.5 active coils, since more coils exceed a new part's time budget (spec 6.3 §6.6).

Compared in part, each table's first line says which rows: DIN 5419 (five single sizes from product pages, six typed), Ring Magnet (five sizes from makers' pages, five typed, no standard), Threaded Coupling 3000 (not NPS 1/4, 3/8), Clinch Nut (not M6, M8), Hex Standoff (no standard, makers differ), Ball Valve (the body diameter is typed), V-Belt Pulley (the plain bore is typed; the catalogue's pulleys take taper bushes).

**Not compared:** Metric O-Ring (common trade sizes beside ISO 3601), and, typed for trying out their shapes and placement: Clinch Stud, Cage Nut (only its 9.5 mm square hole was confirmed), Extension Spring, Tube Cap, Bridge Handle, Levelling Foot, Butt Hinge. None of these has one standard; a maker's table is what they need.

Bearing boundary dimensions follow ISO 15, so makers agree on them.

T-slot profiles (2026-11-10, spec 6.1 §3.11a): the section's outside, grid, slot opening, groove depth and core are the
published ones where the table above says so; a lip is the item data sheet's for slot 8 and **typed** for slots 5, 6 and 10
(2.0, 2.0, 4.0), the groove depths of slots 6 and 10 (9, 15) and the cores of slots 5, 6 and 10 (tap drills of M5, M6, M12)
are typed, the corner radii are typed. The chamber's width and the cavities are then sized so the area is the maker's;
the maker's own inner webs are not modelled, so Ix and It are ours. Not compared: Ix against the makers' values.

DIN 508 T-slot nuts are for machine-table slots (DIN 650), not for profile slots: `M6 x 8` fits the slot 8 profile (neck 8 in
the 8 mm opening, foot 6 high in a 7.75 mm chamber), the others stand 2 mm proud of a profile's face. Profile slot nuts,
brackets, end caps and feet for these systems wait for a maker's published table (spec 6.3 §6.5c).

Rexroth strut profiles (2026-11-10): the published chamber, opening, lip, groove depth and core make the slots; one wall
thickness for the outer walls and webs is then sized so the area is the catalogue's (every size within 3 %; 30x30 is 2 % over at the thinnest wall, 0.8 mm), so Ix and Iy are ours
(the test prints both; Ix comes out 7 to 28 % under the catalogue's: 40x40L 8.4 / 9.1, 45x45L 10.1 / 11.7, 45x45 13.2 / 18.3, 90x90 266 / 300 cm4). The slot 10 lip (3) and the slot 8 undercut depth (where the floor is as wide as the opening) are typed, and
the core tube is the bore plus 6 mm. Left out: 20x20 (slot 6: no slot drawing read), 30x60 and 80x120L (their areas need inner
shapes the model does not have). Rexroth's connectors (T-nuts, brackets, caps, feet) are not built: the catalogue gives their
sizes only in drawings, which the text of these pages does not carry readably. Their CAD files were not used.

Fasteners 2026-11-10: lengths marked "typed" follow the standard's length series between the range ends we know for the size, not
a table compared row by row (DIN 7984, ISO 10642 M16 and M20, ISO 13337, ISO 1234, ISO 8735, ISO 4762 / 4017 / 4014 M14). ISO 7090
uses ISO 7089's sizes (its chamfer is left out). There is no "ISO 7984": the low head socket screw is DIN 7984.

**Not used:** MISUMI. Its terms for CAD data forbid obtaining it in bulk and reusing it beyond designing with its
products; its catalogue is not a source for this library unless MISUMI agrees.
